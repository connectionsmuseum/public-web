# Seattle content and exhibit source audit

Reviewed 2026-10-25; route map updated 2026-10-26. This is a guide to what our draft covers and what deserves another pass, not a fixed inventory of everything visitors will see. The large switches stay in place; smaller displays move, and volunteers keep adding working functions. Confirm current display and operating status with the museum before making specific promises.

## Represented in this branch

- `/seattle.html` is a short overview with routes into the visit guide, exhibits, and library. Its `#visit-info`, `#exhibits`, and `#library` anchors remain useful for incoming links. The JKL collection update and Press links remain here; the two Seattle Times article bodies remain inaccessible to the web reader.
- `/seattle/visiting-info.html` holds hours, directions, tours, access, safety, and group guidance. It combines the earlier ground-level entrance and spoken-tour details with Sarah's narrow-switch-aisle and break information, plus advance contact for groups larger than six and possible tours outside public hours.
- `src/data/seattleVisit.ts` supplies shared Seattle hours, location, donations, contact details, and an optional dated closure notice to the home, Seattle overview, and visit pages. The home page keeps its distinct mobile callout wording and layout while reading those facts from the same file.
- `/seattle/exhibits.html` is the exhibit overview. It keeps the invitation to explore and ask volunteer docents questions alongside Sarah's founding timeline and hands-on restoration details, then points readers to the switch, Panel, and other exhibit stories.
- `/seattle/exhibits/switches.html` follows a call to compare the third-floor mechanical machines with the second-floor electronic ones. It distinguishes the No. 1 and No. 5 Crossbars, names the workers who built and restored them, and includes the No. 5's Adams office origin and renewed coin-call functions. It also covers Panel, step-by-step, PBXs, 3ESS, DMS-10, power, and ringing.
- `/seattle/exhibits/other.html` gives payphones and teletypes more space. It covers the K-6 call box, the teletypes' news-wire and testboard roles, and the Space Needle cordless phone. Outside plant remains a short supporting note.
- `/seattle/exhibits/panel.html` expands the Panel's 1923 dial-service debut, its sender-based call setup and test desk, the move through Parkway's window, and the volunteers who rebuilt it. The route uses the existing generic page and section components.
- `/seattle/library.html` holds the reference library's purpose, research contact, and contribution guidance. The Seattle library is described as cataloged on THG's overview page; confirm any access procedure before adding hours or a public catalog link.
- The exhibit introduction names the Telephone Pioneers' work moving and reconnecting the switch frames. Copy also explains that smaller displays and functions change, so it does not read like a permanent catalog.

## Strong next stories

1. **Payphone and teletype object stories.** The [older telephone exhibit](https://www.telcomhistory.org/seattleTelephones.html) tells the K-6 call box's move in detail; the current payphone collection page is sparse. The [teletype inventory](https://wiki.connections.museum/w/CMS:Teletype_Collection) names particular machines and a patchable testboard. Ask volunteers which machines can be demonstrated now and which teletype appeared in _Argo_ before adding a model number or photo caption. The film connection in visitor copy comes directly from the museum volunteer's account, not the old THG page.
2. **An illustrated two-floor route.** The [museum inventory](https://wiki.connections.museum/w/CMS:Connections_Museum) supports the floor sequence. A map or a few photos could help visitors compare relays, circuit cards, and computer hardware without implying every call physically passes through all the exhibits.
3. **Images and demonstrations.** The repo has a Panel photo but lacks current images for payphones, teletypes, 3ESS, and DMS-10. Photograph what visitors can actually see now, credit the photographer, and describe what can be demonstrated.
4. **Other equipment when the core stories are settled.** THG's [other-equipment page](https://www.telcomhistory.org/seattleOther.html) covers amateur radio, a 1928 Western Electric AM transmitter from Everett, a sound-film projector, a sewing machine, a washer, and a wire recorder. Check what is currently displayed before giving these objects visitor-page space.

## Claims to check before adding more copy

- THG's electronic page says the 3ESS could serve up to 4,500 lines and describes the 5ESS and electronic key systems; it also marks its 1ESS as **not on display**. Do not present those other systems as running Seattle exhibits solely on the strength of an older online page.
- The older [electronic switching page](https://www.telcomhistory.org/seattleElectronic.html) says the Parkway Panel was replaced in 1972, while both the older [mechanical page](https://www.telcomhistory.org/seattleSwitching.html) and the [Panel wiki page](https://wiki.connections.museum/w/CMS:Panel_Switch) say 1974. Visitor copy says "into the 1970s"; confirm the exact retirement/cutover date with the equipment team.
- Sarah's draft says the museum was founded in 1984, while the [museum timeline](https://wiki.connections.museum/w/CMS:Connections_Museum) dates Warrick's proposal to 1984, the start of work to 1986, and early tours to 1988. Visitor copy distinguishes planning from construction. Her unfinished special-tour donation line, the filming and audio-recording policy request, and the payphone-signage placeholder are retained beside the relevant source sections as commented TODOs; none is published as visitor-facing copy yet.
- The visitor page mentions blue boxing as a subject to ask a volunteer docent about, without promising a demonstration on every visit. Check current operating and supervision arrangements before offering more specific instructions.
- Checked 2026-10-26: The [JKL Museum updates](https://www.jklmuseum.com/jkl-museum-of-telephony-news-and-updates/) say its collection moved to Seattle and the new display gallery is not yet open for visitors. A museum volunteer supplied the current Sarah Autumn and Remco Enthoven staff titles. The supplied [JKL announcement video](https://youtu.be/iXMSm86jxaA?si=5-YijQ4iJTzdz9vf) is linked on the site; YouTube blocked direct reading in the web reader.
- The two Seattle Times article bodies remain inaccessible to the web reader; their URLs were provided by a museum volunteer and are linked without article-derived summaries.
