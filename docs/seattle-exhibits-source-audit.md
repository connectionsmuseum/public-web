## Detail worth preserving or checking

- The [electronic exhibit](https://www.telcomhistory.org/seattleElectronic.html) describes the 3ESS as a community dial office switch for smaller communities, with capacity for 4,500 lines. It describes the 5ESS as a modular Class 4 or Class 5 switch. Its electronic key telephone system history runs from the Dimension to the System 75 and System 85. These are useful stories about shrinking central offices and changing workplace phones, but the page mixes history with statements phrased as current; recheck those before bringing them into visitor copy.
- The same electronic page marks the 1ESS as **not on display**. Its claim about when the Parkway Panel was replaced does not match the [spring 2023 THG newsletter](https://www.telcomhistory.org/newsletters/spring2023.pdf), so leave replacement dates out until the museum confirms them.
- The [outside plant exhibit](https://www.telcomhistory.org/seattleOutside.html) explains that a telephone circuit uses a pair of wires. Its building terminals span about 1910 onward; open-wire lead appeared in Seattle by 1883; and the splice display ranges from paper-insulated copper to fiber, including a 4,200-pair copper splice. These examples make the outdoor work and its scale tangible.
- The overview also mentions **central plant** equipment, which the current draft does not explain. Confirm which power, test, or support equipment is on public view before adding a card.

## Good candidates for later branches

1. **Mechanical switching deep dive.** Verify installation and service dates for the Panel and No. 1 Crossbar, then explain how a call travels through each. Preserve the workers' role in moving, reconnecting, repairing, and teaching these systems.
2. **Telephone and station equipment.** Check the older site's [telephone exhibit](https://www.telcomhistory.org/seattleTelephones.html) and the museum inventory before writing individual cards for picturephones, payphones, Design Line sets, and the Space Needle radio-telephone currently named in the preexisting draft.
3. **Telegraphs, teletypes, and other equipment.** Review the older [telegraph/teletype](https://www.telcomhistory.org/seattleTelegraphs.html) and [other equipment](https://www.telcomhistory.org/seattleOther.html) pages, then pick specific objects with good photos and demonstrable stories.
4. **Exhibit images.** Source and credit photographs of the actual terminal, open-wire, splice, PBX, and 3ESS displays. The current repo has a panel photo but no image files for those exhibits.

The mechanical switching, telephone, telegraph/teletype, and other-equipment subpages were unavailable to the web reader during this review. The browser access request was also denied by automatic safety review, so this branch does not claim to have inspected their text. Avoid filling their cards from guesses. The Panel description here also draws on [THG's spring 2023 newsletter](https://www.telcomhistory.org/newsletters/spring2023.pdf); its date and mechanism should be checked against the equipment team before publishing.
